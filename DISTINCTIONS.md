## Problem-fit pathways

These combine the evidence above into specific proposed uses. Each includes the next demonstration and an outcome measure.

### Human intent into inspectable systems

Build across the gaps between what a person means, what a system encodes, what an agent does, and what a user understands.

**Problem:** A tool-assisted workflow loses meaning between conversation, configuration, execution and handoff.

**Proof to put in front of someone:** One bounded workflow with source statements, structured requirements, acceptance tests and a replayable result.

**Measure:** Measure task completion, incorrect handoffs, human correction time and unresolved ambiguity against a stated baseline.

### Creative practice that can also build its instruments

Connect released artistic work, expressive domain models, and audience-facing publication infrastructure.

**Problem:** The making, organizing and presenting of a creative body of work become disconnected.

**Proof to put in front of someone:** A credited artwork paired with a functioning tool that shows exactly how composition or interpretation informed its design.

**Measure:** Document participant use, creative control, accessibility and repeat engagement; evaluate with an explicit artistic brief.

### Systems people can understand and use

Combine executable structures with precise explanations and public-facing experiences.

**Problem:** A system is technically available but its intended users do not understand what to do or why.

**Proof to put in front of someone:** A task-based onboarding sequence with a working example, a rubric and independently attempted completion.

**Measure:** Compare unaided completion, time-to-first-success and recurring support questions before and after.

### Research through making and testing representations

Turn an interpretive question into a domain model, an executable artifact and a testable investigation.

**Problem:** A cross-disciplinary question needs both conceptual clarity and an instrument for investigating it.

**Proof to put in front of someone:** A bounded research question, artifact, comparison with prior art, evaluation protocol and public methods record.

**Measure:** Report results and limitations against prespecified questions; distinguish creative contribution from validated research novelty.
